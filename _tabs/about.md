# The Mission: Fighting Entropy at Scale

Hi, I'm Alex Perkins. 

I design, automate, and govern modern software-defined control planes for mission-critical physical and virtual infrastructure. For the past 15+ years, I have lived at the intersection of physical connectivity and software-defined reliability. 

My engineering philosophy is rooted in a single principle: **Complex systems naturally degrade into disorder (entropy) unless guided by deterministic automation.** I use tools like Terraform, GitOps pipelines, and robust observability suites not just to deploy code, but to enforce structural integrity on complex environments.

My approach to infrastructure didn't come from a textbook; it was forged through real-world execution across high-stakes domains:

## 🌌 Why Physics?

People often ask why an infrastructure engineer is spending years studying quantum mechanics, thermodynamics, and multivariable calculus. 

To me, physics is the ultimate "first-principles" framework. A massive energy grid, a Kubernetes cluster, and a planetary ecosystem are all governed by the same fundamental laws of system dynamics, capacity, and information flow. 

By looking at software infrastructure through a mathematical lens, I can spot logical inconsistencies, predict systemic bottlenecks, and build automated "cooling mechanisms" (like declarative CI/CD pipelines) that standard engineering paradigms often miss.
